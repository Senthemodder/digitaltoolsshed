      "a": "Induced Gas Flotation (IGF) is a rapid physical-chemical separation process used offshore and in refineries to treat oily produced water before ocean disposal or reinjection. Produced water passes through 3 to 4 sequential chambers. In each chamber, an eductor jet or high-speed self-inducing impeller draws blanket gas (natural gas or nitrogen) into the water stream, shearing it into billions of microbubbles (30 to 90 μm). Microbubbles collide with and adhere to suspended oil droplets, forming low-density oil-gas aggregates that rise rapidly to the water surface where rotating skim paddles push the oily froth into a collection trough."

      "q": "Why is modified Stokes law crucial for IGF design compared to API gravity separators?",
      "a": "In conventional API gravity separators or skim tanks, tiny oil droplets (10 to 30 μm) settle upward very slowly under pure gravity (Stokes rise velocity <0.1 mm/s), requiring hours of residence time. In an IGF, attachment of multiple gas bubbles reduces the aggregate density from ~850 kg/m³ to under 300 kg/m³, while doubling the effective diameter. According to Stokes law, rise velocity scales with diameter squared and density difference, boosting rise rates by 20 to 50 times (up to 3 to 10 mm/s) and reducing required residence time to just 3 to 5 minutes."
